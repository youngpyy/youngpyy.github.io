The impact of reference prices on consumer purchase behavior and firm profit in the context of multiple alternatives is well-established and extensively studied. However, as product quality awareness increases and information becomes readily available due to advanced technology, consumers often base their purchase decisions on a multi-attribute rather than a single-attribute reference point (Tereyağoğlu et al. 2018). This study investigates a firm’s product quality and pricing optimization problem under a common price cap when considering the multi-attribute reference effect in consumer choice behavior. To this end, we introduce a new multi-attribute reference-dependent choice model that includes reference quality and price, specifically in the worst-case attributes form. Furthermore, we examine two practical operations management issues: (i) assortment and quality optimization with given prices, mirroring the manufacturer’s series-product design, and (ii) the retailer’s decision on assortment and price. For each issue, we not only characterize the structure of the optimal strategy and propose an efficient algorithm but also examine the relationship between the optimal solutions and reference effect sensitivities. Finally, we conduct a numerical study using a real data set to evaluate model fit and predictive performance and illustrate operational implications. As an extension of the research, we also discuss other reference point formations, including the best and mixed cases, indicating the importance of the multi-attribute reference point effect in consumer choice and the significance of this paper in offering managerial implications for firms’ operational decisions.

In the post-Moore’s Law era, the semiconductor industry faces a paradox: rapid innovation at advanced nodes coexists with crippling structural shortages at mature legacy nodes (e.g., automotive chips). In this article, we identify a structural crowding-out effect whereby advanced-node products can systematically displace essential legacy capacity when allocation decisions overemphasize nominal profitability under shared bottleneck constraints. To address this, we propose a novel product portfolio optimization framework that endogenizes supply-chain resilience. Departing from compensatory utility models used in retail, we introduce a threshold multinomial logit approach to characterize the noncompensatory nature of business-to-business semiconductor procurement, where production yield acts as a hard qualification barrier. We formulate the problem as a nonconvex linear fractional combinatorial optimization model and develop an exact solution algorithm integrating iterative anchoring with Dinkelbach’s method. Our results reveal that shifting the strategic focus from absolute profit margins to profit efficiency per bottleneck resource not only enhances foundry profitability but also automatically preserves capacity for legacy nodes during disruptions. These findings are supported by an exact globally optimal solution framework and a systematic numerical analysis of the technological and market conditions shaping the portfolio decision. This finding suggests that resilience achieved through algorithmic portfolio optimization, rather than through capacity expansion alone, serves as a critical lever for mitigating global supply-chain vulnerabilities.
